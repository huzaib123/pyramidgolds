Gold coins and gold bars

How to choose between coins, bars and biscuits, what to check on the assay card, and how resale works.

Last updated: 6 November 2026

Why buy gold coins and bars?

Coins and bars are the simplest way to own gold for its metal value. Unlike jewellery, there are no making charges for design work, so more of what you pay goes into the gold itself. They are also easy to price: weight times the current rate, plus a small premium.

Coins, bars and biscuits compared

FormTypical weightsBest for
Gold coin1 g to 50 gGifts for weddings, nikah, Eid and Diwali; starting small
Gold bar10 g to 1 kgLarger savings at a lower premium per gram
Gold biscuit100 g; 10 tola (116.64 g)Traditional holding; simple to store and resell

What to check before you buy

  • Purity. Investment gold is usually 999.9 or 999 fine (24 karat). The purity should be printed on the piece and its card.
  • Brand and packaging. A recognised refiner, such as MMTC-PAMP, in a sealed, tamper-proof assay card.
  • Serial number. It should match between the bar and its card.
  • Invoice. Ask for a GST invoice that shows weight, purity and the rate used.
  • Buy-back terms. Ask how the shop buys back the piece, and what condition it needs to be in.

Frequently asked questions

Is it better to buy gold coins or gold bars?

Coins suit small budgets and gifts. Bars carry a lower premium per gram, so they suit larger, long-term purchases. Many buyers hold both.

How many grams is one tola?

One tola is about 11.66 g. A 10 tola biscuit weighs 116.64 g.

Should I keep the assay card sealed?

Yes. A sealed assay card with an intact serial number makes the piece quick to verify and easier to sell back.
