Gold jewellery buying guide

Seven things to check before you buy gold jewellery, from the karat and hallmark to making charges and the invoice.

Last updated: 6 November 2026

1. Decide on the karat

The karat tells you how much of the piece is gold. 22 karat (22K916) is 91.6% gold and is the usual choice for traditional and bridal jewellery. 18 karat (18K750) is 75% gold; it is harder, holds stones securely and suits lighter, modern designs. Lower karats are more durable but contain less gold.

2. Check the hallmark

Look for the BIS mark, the purity grade and the 6-character HUID, then verify the HUID in the BIS CARE app. Our guide to understanding hallmarked gold walks through it step by step. Every gold and silver piece at Pyramid Golds carries official hallmark certification.

3. Understand how the price is built

A fair quote for gold jewellery should show each part of the price:

  • Gold rate for the karat you are buying, on the day.
  • Net gold weight, excluding any stones.
  • Making charges for design and craftsmanship.
  • Stone charges, if the piece is set with stones.
  • GST, shown on the invoice.

Because the gold rate moves through the day, we do not post prices online. Message us on WhatsApp with the design or weight and we reply with an exact quote.

4. Think about wear and occasion

  • Bridal and nikah: necklace sets, nath, maang tikka and bangles, usually in 22 karat.
  • Everyday wear: light chains, studs, hoops and slim bracelets that will not snag.
  • Gifts: earrings, rings or a gold coin, which is easy to choose without knowing a size.

5. Get the right size

For rings and bangles, bring a piece that already fits, or visit the store so we can measure. Sizing is one of the most common reasons to come in rather than order by message.

6. Keep the invoice

Always take a GST invoice that lists the weight, purity, making charges and HUID. It is your proof of purchase and makes exchange or resale simpler. Every purchase at Pyramid Golds comes with a full GST invoice.

7. Silver jewellery

Silver is a good option for daily wear and gifting on a smaller budget. Ask to see the fineness mark, such as 925 for sterling silver. We carry hallmarked silver jewellery such as bracelets and bangles.

Frequently asked questions

Which karat is best for gold jewellery?

22 karat is the usual choice for traditional and bridal jewellery. 18 karat is harder, so it holds stones better and suits lighter, modern designs.

What are making charges?

Making charges pay for the design and craftsmanship. They vary with how detailed the piece is, so ask for them to be shown separately on the quote and the invoice.
